# Source Packet: Agent Economics

## Brief

- Audience: agent-runtime architects, economists of computation, and governance designers.
- Promise: explain why compute, memory, attention, and bandwidth become governance questions in persistent agent systems.
- Angle: budgets are not merely cost controls; they shape priority, fairness, and agency.
- Series role: examine resource pressure inside the polis without claiming a finished market.

## Claim Posture

- Current: ADL treats resource limits and evidence-bearing cost as governance concerns in multiple runtime and planning surfaces.
- Historical/planned: markets, bidding, trade, and inter-polis economics are design proposals, not current production behavior.
- Unknown: which economic mechanism would produce desirable long-run outcomes.

## Article Shape

1. Persistent agents consume scarce resources.
2. Compute, memory, attention, and bandwidth as governed goods.
3. Budgets, priorities, and policy overrides.
4. Why markets are a proposal, not a default answer.
5. The research questions that must remain open.

## Guardrails

Do not claim a deployed agent market, token economy, payment network, x402 integration, measured efficiency gains, or settled welfare model.
